# Finite Transfers and Open Energy Balances

Exact controls and decisive measurements for gears, windings, and their supplies.

A self-contained companion to
[*Frames, Returns, and Port Power*](https://github.com/hobnilre/physics-gear/blob/main/frames-returns-and-port-power.pdf).
The article develops exact finite comparisons for 46 open energy questions.

## What this article adds, and why it matters

Where does the work go when a receiver is attached to an orbiting gear?
How does moving a transformer return change the energy delivered to a load?
How much energy can remain hidden behind a zero terminal signal, and what
determines its destination when a contact opens or a switch changes state?
The article brings these questions together through exact derivations and
proposed measurements of each transfer and stored-energy change.

The worked models give concrete separations: a 7 J change in carrier work,
different heat deliveries at identical switching endpoints, and a finite
actuator that changes a receiver's final energy. Each result leads to an
experimental energy question:

- **Attaching a receiver changes the whole train's work.** In the loaded
  gear control, carrier work changes from −7 J to zero in one second while
  three receivers take 7 J between them. The article derives the changed
  shaft reactions and shared axle transfers. What additional work appears
  through the supports, bearings, transverse joint forces, and phase drive
  of a physical lead-out?

- **Large circulating powers and frame-dependent work.** In one compound train,
  the ratio of largest observed port power to ground-frame throughput changes
  from 1 to $2950/21$ between ground and carrier observations.
  Different preparation histories also
  change the division of work between shafts and effective fields while
  reaching the same endpoint states. The open measurement is how each
  reconstructed work, physical support transfer, and receiver delivery
  compares on the same loaded motion.

- **An electrical return becomes an energy-transfer path.** The conducting
  attachment control requires $5/6$ J of source work and retains $1/2$ J.
  In a separate reference-drive control, finite actuator response changes
  the receiver endpoint from 2 J to $(9+e^{-10})^2/50$ J. Moving returns,
  probe loading, and reference supplies make the gear–transformer
  correspondence a question about delivered work and evolving stores.

- **Hidden energy behind identical readouts.** Two prepared electrical modes
  carry $1/1000000$ J while their terminal currents cancel exactly. Another
  preparation family retains the same differential oscillation while its
  stored energy ranges from $95/2$ to $115/2$ J and its coil work changes
  direction. Internal branch measurements and preparation-source work can
  reveal energy changes that a terminal readout leaves hidden.

- **The same final state can send energy to different destinations.** Reversing
  two charge-sharing stages exchanges their heat deliveries of $3/8$ and
  $3/32$ J without changing the final capacitor state. Two energized-opening
  clamp laws instead deliver $3/2$ and 2 J. Actual switch conduction, arc
  ignition, clamp release, and contact restitution determine which path a
  physical apparatus follows and where each transfer lands.

- **Material states and supplies carry their own energy questions.** The
  magnetic lag control converts $\pi/2$ J per cycle into heat; the finite sensor
  retains $1/8$ J. A supplied transducer receives 1 J of actuator work while
  its controller and bias draw 3 J. A separate source-off return transfers
  $1/2$ J back to a supply rail through a mechanical input. Following these
  stores through preparation, operation, and reset puts the complete cycle
  within reach of independent measurements.

- **Signed discrepancies that survive independent checks.** A specified phase
  error changes a zero work reading to $-\pi/2$ J. The article derives exact
  bounds for channel products and endpoint stores, and gives a known-energy
  input for checking the measurement chain. Which positive or negative
  residuals remain after polarity, timing, loading, calibration, and stored
  energy have each been established independently?

The closing comparisons specify what to hold, drive, and measure, with the
different energy predictions side by side. Unresolved transfers retain their
sign, magnitude, operating conditions, and completed checks as targets for
the next experiment.
